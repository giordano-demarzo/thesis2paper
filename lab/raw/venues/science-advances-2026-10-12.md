## Journal description (Information for authors, intro)
- Described as a "highly selective, open access journal" publishing original research and reviews across a broad range of disciplines.
- Editorial Board (active researchers) evaluates submissions and sends those warranting further consideration to external peer review.
- No presubmission inquiries; submit the complete manuscript at https://cts.sciencemag.org.
- Open access, funded by APCs (see licensing and charges page).

## Types of manuscripts
- **Research Articles**: "should present a major advance." Flexible format, short or long, **up to 15,000 words**, **up to 10 figures and/or tables (combined)**, and **80 references**.
- **Research Resources**: validated technique/tool/database without substantial new-insight analysis; same format as Research Articles (may omit the Results heading); deputy editors decide on the tag; same review process.
- **Reviews**: up to 10,000 words, up to 10 figures/tables, no more than 150 references (more on request); generally solicited, unsolicited considered.
- Focus articles and editorials are commissioned.

## Preparing a new manuscript
- Use the Science Advances main paper template and the Supplementary Materials template.
- Language editing services listed (Accdon LetPub, Edanz, Editage, Enago); not endorsed, no influence on acceptance.
- Health research: follow EQUATOR reporting guidelines.

## Submission form (portal)
- Authors tab: ORCID required for first and corresponding authors; strongly encouraged for all.
- Manuscript info: article type; **title max 135 characters**; **short title max 50 characters**; one or two field codes.
- Cover letter must include: title; why appropriate for Science Advances (what is new, how it advances the field); info for fair review incl. related manuscripts elsewhere; statement not published/under consideration elsewhere; conflicts of interest; human informed consent statement if applicable; animal care statement if applicable; where all data are/will be available and any restrictions (e.g., MTA); info on auxiliary files; suggestion of a Deputy/Section Editor and an Associate Editor; all funding sources.
- Reviewers: must provide **five** potential reviewers (name, affiliation, email); may list up to **three** excluded reviewers. Diversity of gender and country encouraged.
- Upload: cover letter (PDF/.docx); combined PDF (manuscript + figures + tables + SM) for evaluation; manuscript in .docx (preferred) or LaTeX; auxiliary files; SM as single PDF.
- Auxiliary files: related papers by any subset of authors under consideration/in press elsewhere; data files needed for review; written permission for cited "in press" work by non-authors; relevant MTAs. "In press" citations not allowed in accepted manuscripts.

## Formatting an initial manuscript
- Single .docx, or LaTeX + PDF with supporting files, components in order: title, main text, references, table/figure legends, list of SM contents.
- "There is no required format for initial submission." Specific formatting may be required at revision.

### Research Articles — main text sections
Title (≤135 characters); Short title (≤50 characters); Authors and Affiliations; Abstract; Introduction; Results; Discussion; Materials and Methods; References; Acknowledgments.
- Tables and figures embedded in text at first mention, with legends.

### Authors and affiliations
- First name, middle initial, last name, comma-separated; designate first, corresponding, equal-contribution authors beneath title in a single paragraph; affiliations via superscript numbers.

### Abstract
- **No more than 150 words**, single paragraph, for a general readership, no citations.
- Suggested structure: general intro sentence to the field; more specific background sentence; brief objectives/methods; key results; final sentence with main conclusions.
- Define in the abstract any abbreviation appearing in the title. No graphical abstracts.

### Introduction
- States purpose, scope, and scientific question; background should give context to readers in other disciplines.

### Results
- Divided into subsections by experimental theme; subheads are descriptive phrases. Define technical terms and abbreviations at first use. Cite tables/figures in numerical order.
- All data must be in main text, SM, or an established database with accession details in Acknowledgments.

### Discussion
- Conclusions from results, significance and implications; must include a paragraph on the study's limitations.

### Materials and Methods
- Experimental design and objectives, distinguishing pre-specified hypotheses from post hoc ones; statistical methods in enough detail for a knowledgeable reader with the data to verify results; N, P, and specific test per experiment in figure legend or main text. Subheads are descriptive phrases.
- (Placement: after Discussion, before References. No separate word limit for Methods stated on the page.)

### References and citations
- One combined list for main text and SM, appended to the main text.
- In-text numeric in parentheses: (1); (2, 3); (4–6). Numbered by order: text, then figure/table captions and boxes, then SM-only references.
- No op. cit./ibid./et al. in place of full author lists; complete format lists all authors, full article title, journal abbreviation, volume, first and last page, year.
  - Example form: "N. Tang, On the equilibrium partial pressures of nitric acid and ammonia in the atmosphere. Atmos. Environ. 14, 819-834 (1980)."
- "In press" allowed at initial submission but must be published before publication. Personal communications and working papers not acceptable references.
- Footnotes and endnotes are not allowed.
- Preprint form: "K. Abe et al., http://arXiv.org/abs/hep-ex/0107061 (2001)" (if published, give standard reference instead).

### Acknowledgments
- Immediately after References; include: complete funding info; each author's contribution by initials; competing interests statement; data and materials availability statement (incl. MTAs).
- Standard competing interests text: "The authors declare that they have no competing interests."
- Standard data statement: all data needed to evaluate the conclusions are present in the paper and/or the SM; list repositories and access codes if applicable.
- Standard no-funding statement provided on the page.

### Figures and tables
- **Up to a total of ten figures and/or tables (combined)**. Number in order of citation. Embedded in the Word file for initial submission (legend below).
- Tables: boldface label and title; no footnotes in column heads; legends **may not exceed 250 words**.
- Universal fonts (e.g., Times New Roman).
- No digital enhancement/manipulation of images; linear adjustments to whole image only; nonlinear adjustments specified in legend.

### Equations
- Display equations on own line and numbered; not punctuated. Legacy equation editor or MathType recommended; avoid Word 2007+ equation editor. LaTeX submitters must check equation conversion.

### Supplementary Materials
- Auxiliary info: datasets, movies, audio, additional figures/tables. Each element with brief title and description; prepare PDF with SM template.
- Supplementary Text: control/supplemental experiments etc. that bear directly on main arguments; "Further discussion or development of arguments beyond those in the main text is not permitted."
- Figures S1, S2…; Tables S1, S2…; large tables as Excel.
- SM-only references go at the end of the main reference list; no separate SM reference list.
- SM text/tables/figures as a single PDF **not exceeding 25 MB**; auxiliary/movie files limited to **10 files total, combined ≤ 25 MB**.
- Movies: .mp4 preferred, HD 1920x1080 where possible; no animated GIFs. Audio: WAV/AIFF/AU/m4a preferred; MP3/AAC ≥160 kb/s.

### Revised manuscripts
- Must follow the Science Advances template; figures follow the Guide to Preparing Figures; use topical checklist (Social and Interdisciplinary Sciences; Neuroscience; Earth/Environmental/Ecological/Space; Physical and Materials; Biomedicine and Life Sciences).
- Revised submission considered final. Point-by-point response uploaded as separate document.

### Licensing / other
- License to Publish; choose CC BY-NC or CC BY.
- Resubmissions only when explicitly invited. Appeals within 4 weeks; desk rejections not appealable.

## LaTeX (Preparing submissions using LaTeX, Science family)
- Science-family journals prefer .docx; LaTeX allowed, especially for math-heavy papers; template not intended for beginners.
- Production converts .tex at acceptance; follow instructions to avoid delays.
- Template: science_template_v1.1.zip, "Version 1.1, updated April 2026". Rename science_template.tex.
- Do not add \usepackage beyond the template; keep custom commands simple in the dedicated preamble section; one .tex file only (no \include/\input); place figures/tables in reserved locations.
- References: one combined list (no separate SM list); use \cite{}; BibTeX recommended; keep .aux/.bbl; for revisions paste .bbl into .tex or upload it.
- Uploads: Combined PDF, plus split Manuscript PDF and Supplementary Material PDF; at revision also separate figure files and a .zip/.tar.gz of LaTeX sources as "Other Supporting Files".

## Editorial policies (Science journals) — relevant excerpts
- Authorship: ICMJE-informed criteria (incl. "creation of new software used in the work"); AI tools cannot be authors. Authors of accepted papers explain contributions; CRediT model encouraged in acknowledgments.
- Competing interests: disclose interests within 5 years of research inception; summarized in a Competing interests statement in acknowledgments. Funding statement in Acknowledgments.
- AI use: AI-assisted technologies used in research or writing must be noted in the cover letter and in methods or acknowledgments; AI-generated images not permitted without editor permission.
- Prior publication: theses/dissertations not prior publication; preprints (arXiv etc.) supported; peer-reviewed citable conference proceedings count as prior publication; don't post revised versions responding to review while under consideration.
- TOP guidelines: data, code, methods cited with DOIs; all data used must be available; "all computer code central to the findings being reported should be available to readers"; author-written code archived in a permanent public repository before publication and cited; materials availability statement in all papers.
- Statistics: detailed reporting requirements (n, tests, alpha, sidedness, multiple-testing corrections, uncertainty measures, full test statistics and P values).
- Modeling studies: computational models validated experimentally or with an independent dataset; assumptions clearly stated.
- Survey studies: disclose sampling design/size, weighting, question wording/order, recruitment, etc., in SM; avoid "representative sample" without stating assumptions.
- Data and code deposition: tabulated data underlying figures archived in machine-readable format (csv/tsv/json/xml); repositories like Dryad, Dataverse, Zenodo; GitHub code must be archived (e.g., Zenodo/Code Ocean) and that version cited; data/code citations go in reference list and are cited in the "Data, code, and materials availability" section of the acknowledgments. Dryad publication cost covered up to 10 GB.
