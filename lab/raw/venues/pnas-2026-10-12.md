## General
- Submissions welcome in all scientific disciplines. No presubmission inquiries.

## Article types (unsolicited)
- **Research Reports**: "describe the results of original research of exceptional importance." **Preferred length 6 pages; maximum 12 pages.** A standard 6-page article is approximately **4,000 words, 50 references, and 4 medium-size graphical elements** (figures and tables).
  - Direct Submissions (most articles) or Contributed (NAS members, two per year).
- **Brief Reports**: observations of immediate impact / concise conceptual advance; immediate open access; **3 pages ≈ 1,600 words** (incl. text, title page, abstract, figure legends) and **15 references**; SI limited to extended methods, essential datasets, videos (no extra tables/figures); Direct Submission only.
- Letters: 500 words, 2 graphical elements, 10 references; within six months of the subject article.
- Opinions: ~1,500–2,000 words excl. references, no abstract.
- By invitation: Commentaries, Inaugural Articles, Front Matter, Perspectives (max 10 pages), Author Responses, Special Features, Colloquium Papers.

## Initial submissions (format-neutral)
- Manuscript does not need strict journal formatting, but must contain: title page (title, author list, classification, keywords); abstract; significance statement; main text; references; figures/tables with legends (may be separate); SI files (may be separate); contact and competing-interest info for all authors; data sharing plans (all data, documentation, and code); funding info and open-access license choice; list of appropriate Editorial Board members, NAS members, and qualified reviewers (**minimum of three each**), brief justification welcome especially for interdisciplinary papers.

## Revised submissions
- Within two months of revision decision or treated as new. Include revised manuscript and point-by-point response; tracked-changes file strongly encouraged; multiple revisions rarely permitted.

## Manuscript templates
- Word and LaTeX (Overleaf) templates for Research article, Brief Report, SI Appendix (links above). LaTeX support through Overleaf.

## Manuscript order (recommended; number all pages starting with title page)
1. Title page
2. Abstract — explain major contributions to the general reader; **no more than 250 words**; any references cited in full within the abstract and also in the text.
3. Significance statement (Direct and Contributed submissions only) — significance at a level understandable to an undergraduate-educated scientist outside the field; **no more than 120 words**.
4. Main text — Introduction; Results; Discussion; Materials and methods (enough detail to repeat the work).
5. Acknowledgments and funding sources (spell out abbreviations; FundRef names; no dedications).
6. References
7. Figure legends

## Title page elements
- Title: brief (**< 135 characters**), descriptive, comprehensible to a broad audience; include studied organism; avoid numbers, acronyms, abbreviations, punctuation, puns.
- Author line: full names; asterisks for co-corresponding; numbered footnotes for equal contribution.
- Affiliations with superscripts; ORCID required for corresponding author (linked in system, not in file).
- Corresponding author contact (email published).
- Preprint: note server(s) and license.
- Classification: major (Physical, Social, or Biological Sciences) + minor category; dual between major categories allowed.
  - Social Sciences minors incl.: Anthropology; Demography; Economic Sciences; Environmental Sciences; Political Sciences; Psychological and Cognitive Sciences; Social Sciences; Sustainability Science.
  - Physical Sciences minors incl.: Applied Mathematics; Computer Sciences; Physics; Statistics; etc.
  - Biological Sciences minors incl.: Ecology; Evolution; Population Biology; Psychological and Cognitive Sciences; Systems Biology; etc.
- Keywords: **at least three**.

## Main text rules
- "Do not include statements of novelty or priority." Avoid lab jargon. SI units. Link only to permanent public repositories (no personal pages). Define field-specific abbreviations at first mention. Style guide: CSE Manual, 8th ed. (2014).

## Publication-ready source files (after acceptance)
- Word, RTF, or LaTeX; no embedded figures/tables; editable equations; footnote symbols order *, †, ‡, §, ¶, #, ||, **, ††, ...
- LaTeX: system runs PDFLaTeX; latest LaTeX2e; XeLaTeX/LuaLaTeX may fail. Upload .cls/.sty/.bib/.bst/.bbl/.ldf as "LaTeX Support File". No nesting/input commands; don't remove template packages. Figures via \includegraphics, uploaded separately; tables inside main .tex. Use **pnas-new.bst**; use Automatic Journal Title Abbreviation package.

## Figures
- High-resolution; sizes approx. small 9 x 6 cm, medium 11 x 11 cm, large 18 x 22 cm. Text 6–12 pt after reduction. TIFF, EPS, PDF, or PPT. RGB.
- PNAS may ask authors to shorten manuscripts exceeding the stated length.
- Raster: 300 ppi (no type), 600–900 ppi (with type), 1000–1200 ppi line art.
- Tables: editable; brief title above, footnotes below; no multipart tables.

## Figure legends
- Multi-panel: first sentence overview; describe each panel. Labeled error bars; SEM vs SD stated; P values etc.; N stated; numerical axes go to 0 except log axes.

## References
- PNAS style, numbered in order of appearance. No unpublished meeting abstracts or "data not shown"; no retracted articles except about the retraction.
- Main-text references in main file; **separate citation list for SI references**; do not cite main-text references in SI and vice versa.
- Full titles; MEDLINE/PubMed journal abbreviations; **more than five authors → first author et al.**; volume (or DOI); page ranges; access date for online sources.
- Examples given for journal articles, datasets, book chapters, preprints (e.g., "arXiv [Preprint] (2008). https://arxiv.org/abs/... (accessed ...)"), proceedings, archived code (Zenodo DOI), theses.

## Supporting information
- Published as provided (not edited/typeset). SI Appendix: single PDF with all text, figures, tables, movie legends, SI references (SI template). Datasets XLSX/RTF/PDF/CSV/GZ/TXT; movies ≤10 MB each; software in native file types.
- "The main text of the paper must stand on its own without the SI." Figures/tables S1, S2...
- If detailed methods are in SI, main-text methods must give enough detail to follow the logic and must reference SI methods. If the paper is fundamentally a new method/technique, methods must be fully described in the main text.

## Editorial and journal policies (excerpts)
- Peer review: Tier 1 Editorial Board (NAS members) — "More than 50% of submissions are declined at initial evaluation." Tier 2 member or guest editor (guest editors for emerging/interdisciplinary fields). Tier 3 at least two independent reviewers. "The acceptance rate is currently 14%." Single-anonymous for Direct Submissions.
- AI: use of AI/LLMs in research must be disclosed in Materials and Methods (or Acknowledgments), naming tool and model/version; AI cannot be an author; AI-generated images not allowed unless subject of the work or permitted.
- Author contributions: specific contributions published as footnote, taken from submission system (e.g., designed research; performed research; contributed new reagents or analytic tools; analyzed data; wrote the paper).
- Competing interests: disclosed at submission in the system; interests within prior 48 months; footnote published.
- Concurrent submission not allowed.
- Design/methods transparency: main text **must contain a Materials and Methods section** with enough information to follow the work; cannot be solely in SI. Preregistration encouraged.
- Materials and data availability: all materials, data, protocols, code and scripts used in the analysis available in a public repository upon publication; restrictions noted at submission; a sharing-plans statement is published with the article; datasets (original or previously published) must be cited in references. Code: make available e.g. via GitHub or Zenodo; maintain the version associated with the paper. Users of public databases should retain the analysed copy and metadata.
- Statistics: analyses on all data; report software source/version; tests, parameters, exact sample sizes, evidence measures.
- Survey and experimental research: state probability vs nonprobability sample; fielding company and dates; mode; AI use in survey workflow; all verbatim questions in SI; nonprobability samples must not be called "representative"; report dropped cases and weighting variables; synthetic respondents share reported.
- Preprints permitted (must be disclosed). Open access option: CC BY-NC-ND or CC BY; all articles free after six months.
- Appeals within three months; appeals based solely on novelty or general interest unlikely to be granted.
